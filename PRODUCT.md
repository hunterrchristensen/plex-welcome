# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hunter's friends and family, invited to his personal Plex server ("rockethunter"). Almost none of them are technical. They open the link on a phone, usually once, usually while standing in front of a TV they're trying to get working. They are not browsing; they have a specific device and a specific problem. Several will be mildly suspicious that this costs money or is somehow a hassle.

The job: get from "Hunter sent me a link" to "I'm watching something" without texting Hunter.

## Product Purpose

Replace the same four support texts Hunter answers every time he shares the server: how do I accept the invite, which app do I install, why can't I find your stuff, and how do I ask for a movie. Success is a friend setting themselves up start to finish and using the request form without asking a single question.

## Brand Personality

Warm, hand-made, unfussy. It should read as a note from Hunter, not a product page. The `rockethunter` mascot (his face on an SLS rocket) sets the register: he is in on the joke, but the instructions themselves are sincere and genuinely useful. Voice is plain-spoken and second-person, contractions throughout, no jargon, no exclamation-mark enthusiasm.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI landing page.** Endless identical rounded cards, an icon above every heading, tiny uppercase tracked labels above every section. The first version of this page drifted here.
- **Corporate SaaS.** No pricing-page energy, no feature grids, no marketing gloss. These are friends, not customers.
- **Cluttered or busy.** A non-technical reader must find their device and move on. Density is a failure.
- **Editorial-magazine affectation.** No display-serif italics, drop caps, or broadsheet rules. This isn't a magazine.

## Design Principles

1. **The reader is mid-task, standing up.** Every section answers one question and gets out of the way. Scannable beats complete.
2. **Reassure before instructing.** The money question and the "this isn't your Plex ads" confusion are the two things that make people give up. Answer them before they're asked.
3. **One filled block on the page.** Visual weight is a budget; spend it on the request CTA. Everything else is type and hairlines.
4. **Cards are the lazy answer.** Sequences get numbers, disclosures get rules, lists get lists.
5. **The joke is in the mascot and the voice, never in the instructions.** Steps stay literal.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, verified rather than assumed. Works with zero JavaScript. All motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. Disclosure uses native `<details>`, so it works with keyboard and screen readers without ARIA. Read primarily on small screens; no horizontal scroll at any width.
